The Problem

Minecraft ships are usually a costume, not a system

Most "ship" mods either hand the player a finished boat to place, or let them build something that looks like a ship but behaves like a pile of blocks with no rules underneath.

The Common Approach

Players pick a ship from a menu, or decorative builds that don't actually sail, take damage, or survive a server restart as one coherent object. Creativity is either boxed in, or purely visual with no gameplay underneath.

What Players Actually Want

To build their own warship out of ordinary blocks — and have the game treat it as a real vessel: something that moves, fights, takes damage in the right places, and is still there when they log back in.

The Solution

The player creates the vessel. The system interprets it.

FleetCraft never asks the player to select "Create Frigate" from a menu. It reads what they actually built, checks whether it qualifies as a working ship, and classifies it automatically.

Technical Depth

Risk → Approach → Result

The core engineering challenges, and how the architecture is designed to address each one.

ChallengeWhy It's DifficultOur Approach
Player-built ship movementMinecraft normally handles blocks and entities completely differently — a build isn't a single movable object by default.Validated construction is converted into a dedicated ship entity that the engine can move and control as one object.
Multiplayer synchronizationDifferent players must see a consistent ship state at the same time, over an unreliable network.Server-authoritative simulation with client-side interpolation for smooth, consistent rendering.
Damage & consequenceA ship has many meaningful components — a single health bar can't represent that.Module-based damage resolution routes impact to the specific hull, mast, rudder, or other component involved.
PersistenceShips must survive world saves, chunk unloading, and players rejoining later.A structured save record — identity, blueprint, damage state, cargo, crew, and location — stored and reconstructed on load.
PerformanceLarge player-built ships can contain a great many blocks and interacting systems at once.Performance optimization is explicitly scheduled as its own stage (v0.8) before polish and release — not an afterthought.

What Are We Actually Building?

FleetCraft is not simply a boat-building mod. It's a gameplay framework that turns player-constructed medieval vessels into persistent, controllable, multiplayer-capable ships.

Investor Summary

The Technical Thesis

FleetCraft's core technical challenge isn't creating a boat model. It's creating a system that can interpret player-created Minecraft structures, validate them, convert them into controllable entities, simulate their behavior, synchronize them in multiplayer, persist their state, and expose all of that through one coherent player experience.
